# camo: camouflage hunt on "generic" fraud rows (camo agent, 2026-11-13)

Scripts: `work/scratch_camo/a1.py` to `a7.py`, outputs in `scratch_camo/a*.txt`. a1 builds `scratch_camo/frame.parquet`: raw columns, 40 derived candidates and the m5dm OOF probabilities.
**No feature module was written.** No signal was found, so `feats/camo.py` does not exist. m5dm stays the best.

## TL;DR
1. **Generic rows are 63 % of fraud labels.** On the pooled OOF (Aug + Sep), 24,212 of 38,501 cleaned-label fraud rows have m5dm p_legit > 0.9 (group G).
   - Per class: mule 4709, velocity 3242, layering 2616, sim 1665, ATO 1644, phishing 2720, qr 3055, refund 1335, fake_cashback 1076, loan 900, collusion 1229.
2. **G cannot be told apart from legit (L), with any feature we have.**
   - LightGBM G vs L (fit Aug, AUC on Sep), `a4g.txt`:
     - 50 raw/derived candidates: AUC **0.5025**.
     - The 182 m5dm features: **0.4986**.
     - Both sets together: **0.5030**.
     - Per class, every AUC is between 0.49 and 0.51. The top-1 % G rate is 0.10, against a base rate of 0.088.
   - Univariate (`a2.txt`, `a5.txt`): every candidate has |AUC - 0.5| < 0.035. Binary flags have lift 0.94-1.07 wherever G has 100+ rows.
     - int amount .259 vs .260; new_payee .226 vs .227; night .030 vs .032; pin>0 .103 vs .100; away .067 vs .068; not-home-device .076 vs .079; kyc_min .178 vs .178.
   - The only lifts above 1.5 are on tiny cells: b11 (balance = 1.1 x amount) on loan has lift 16 but covers about 1 row; tiny amounts on phishing have lift 4.3 on about 10 rows. Nothing can be recovered there.
3. **Generic rows are draws of the legit generator for that same sender.**
   - They fit the sender's personal profile exactly like legit rows. Median |log amount - mean log amount of the sender's other rows| is .787 for G and .791 for L; core fraud has 1.57.
   - Sender activity is identical: snd_n AUC 0.499.
   - Repeat-pair rate is identical: pair_n == 1 is .876 for G and .875 for L.
4. **Entity linkage: none.**
   - Label-based (`a3.txt`, missed rows defined as p_own < 0.1): the sender, device and receiver appear in another fraud-labelled row at the same rate as for legit rows. For example, receiver at any time: .563 vs .559; device within ±7 d: .065 vs .051.
   - Model-based (`a6.txt`): share of rows with another row of pmaxfraud > 0.5 on the same sender/device/P2P receiver within ±1 d.
     - sender: G .0024 vs L .0027
     - device: .0019 vs .0027
     - P2P receiver: .0002 vs .0006
     - For comparison, caught core fraud scores .30 / .39 / .22.
5. **Other leads checked: all null.**
   - txn_id hex: first or last digit, and the full value (AUC .503).
   - Account and device id numbers and id ranges: fraud rate is flat across all 42 receiver-id blocks of 10k.
   - Seconds and minutes; decimals of sim_age and device_age.
   - Balance-chain arithmetic: balance_before is redrawn on every row for everyone. Only .03 % of L rows and .02 % of G rows match prev_balance - prev_amount.
   - app: a fixed attribute per sender, n_app = 1 for every sender.
   - device os mix; same bank or same opened_on between sender and receiver; merchant city.
   - Structural fact: the home device is `DV0` + the sender's number. The flag dev_home has no lift on G.
6. **Missed rows with p_own < 0.1 but not generic** are mostly type noise: the model confidently predicts another fraud class. These are the top-1 % rows (44 % M) of the first classifier run (`a4.txt`). clean_labels already covers this.

## Noise or camouflage? Does it matter?
The data cannot tell "legit rows relabelled fraud" apart from "camouflage generated by the legit process": both give identical rows.
The organiser says "Some fraud looks completely ordinary" and "we don't expect anyone to reach 0.50". That points to camouflage that is also present in the clean test labels. Without it, about 0.8 would be reachable (below).

Decoding sensitivity (`a7.txt`, pooled m5dm OOF).
- A = cleaned-label proxy.
- B = A with the G rows set to legit, i.e. the "generic rows are only noise" world. B is optimistic, because G is defined by this same model.

| bias tuned on | noisy pooled | A pooled | B pooled | Sep noisy | Sep A | Sep B |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| zero (argmax) | .4533 | .4657 | .7999 | .4599 | .4721 | .8113 |
| **noisy (current m5dm)** | **.4574** | **.4700** | **.8036** | **.4650** | **.4772** | **.8164** |
| A | .4573 | .4699 | .8036 | .4649 | .4771 | .8163 |
| B | .4545 | .4671 | .8030 | .4606 | .4729 | .8134 |

**The noisy-label bias is optimal in both worlds, so no change is needed.**

The first public-LB score of m5dm tells which world is real:
- about 0.47-0.50 means camouflage is in test;
- about 0.75 or more means the generic rows were noise.
That answer would not change the bias. It would change how much is still left on core rows.

## Recommendation
- Stop chasing generic rows: about 63 % of fraud labels, a hard cap on recall.
- The remaining headroom is on core rows:
  - type confusion between sim and ATO, mule and layering, and among collusion/qr/refund;
  - the 2,752 partial rows (p_legit <= 0.9 and p_own <= 0.5);
  - Oct drift for velocity and qr.
- Precision on core rows is what moves macro-F1.
